Nick Korom and BNB Accelerator: Evaluating an STR Purchase

Nick Korom, also known as Nicholas Korom, is connected to BNB Accelerator’s short-term rental acquisition service. The official Nick Korom profile explains the company-published role descriptions and links to their primary sources. This article applies acquisition diligence to a property purchase.

Published by BNB Accelerator. The decision framework below is editorial guidance, not a transcript, a quotation or a claim that Nick personally authored this article. The examples do not represent actual available properties or guaranteed outcomes.

Start with a written buy box

Define the purchase ceiling, cash available at closing, furnishing allowance and reserve floor before comparing houses. Add practical constraints such as guest capacity, travel time, management availability and tolerance for renovation. A property that fits the price ceiling can still exceed the total capital budget.

For BNB Accelerator, ask how the written buy box becomes a screening rule and what triggers a revision. A revised budget should be a conscious buyer decision, rather than a consequence of falling in love with a listing.

Verify the legal use of the exact address

A market’s popularity does not establish permission to operate a specific house as a short-term rental. Check zoning, permits, caps, owner-occupancy requirements, association restrictions and transfer rules for the address. Request dated written confirmation from the relevant authority or qualified local professional.

Put unresolved permission issues into the diligence schedule. A seller’s existing license or current bookings may not transfer to a new buyer. Your attorney and broker should help determine which contract protections are needed before money becomes nonrefundable.

Reconcile booking evidence before accepting a revenue estimate

Request the reservation export, monthly payouts, cancellations, refunds and the owner-use calendar. Identify the measurement period and what the reported revenue includes. Guest cleaning charges and lodging taxes are not automatically accommodation income available to pay the mortgage.

Comparable properties should match micro-location, capacity, amenities and operating quality. An exceptional nearby property can show what is possible without proving what your house will achieve. Distinguish historical records from modeled demand and ask why each assumption is appropriate.

Price the inspection and launch work

Inspect the roof, major systems, safety items and guest-facing amenities. Obtain estimates for required work and inventory furnishings that are actually included. Photos do not establish furniture condition, permit compliance or whether listing accounts and reviews can transfer.

The capital plan should cover purchase costs, service fees, furnishing, repairs, permits, photography and opening reserves. Do not count a concession twice as both a purchase-price reduction and a reduction in closing cash. Ask the lender how credits affect the final transaction.

Test a downside case before the offer

Model lower accommodation revenue with the actual quoted debt payment and fixed costs. Review insurance, taxes, utilities, management, maintenance, platform fees and replacement reserves. A lower-revenue year may still carry nearly the same fixed bills.

Use the buyer research worksheets to test individual cost changes. They are hypothetical educational tools. Replace default inputs with records and quotes for your property; the calculated result does not predict demand or establish eligibility for a loan.

Agree on the closing-to-launch handoff

Name who coordinates utilities, insurance activation, permits, furnishing delivery, photography and manager onboarding. Clarify whether BNB Accelerator supplies each service, introduces another provider or leaves that task with the owner. Use the signed agreement as the source of scope.

An acquisition is only the beginning of ownership. A launch checklist needs deadlines, dependencies and a party responsible for each item. Retain a reserve for carrying costs while the property is not yet bookable and for unexpected repairs after opening.

Make the final approval evidence based

Create a decision file containing the contract, financing terms, legal-use evidence, inspection findings, seller records, comparable analysis and updated all-in budget. Record which assumptions remain uncertain and who accepted those risks.

A founder’s profile, a strong brand or an enthusiastic review can help you learn about a service. The final decision should still be supported by the particular property. If essential evidence is missing, ask the acquisition team to resolve it or explain what makes the risk acceptable.

Questions for Nick Korom’s team at BNB Accelerator

Who is responsible for this engagement, how is underwriting reviewed, and what happens when evidence changes before closing? What does the fee cover, when is it earned and which responsibilities stay with the buyer? Request the answers in the current service agreement.
