Short-Term Rental For Sale Checklist: What to Request Before You Offer

Published 2026-01-01 · BNB Accelerator Editorial Team

The financial documents that matter most

Before writing an offer, request twelve to twenty-four months of platform-generated revenue exports (not a seller's spreadsheet), a breakdown of cleaning, supply and management costs, and the property's tax and insurance history.

If the listing is new to short-term rental use and has no platform history, ask for comparable-market data instead -- occupancy and ADR for similar properties nearby -- so the projection is grounded in real performance rather than an optimistic pro forma.

The compliance and physical documents that matter next

Confirm short-term rental permit or license status directly with the local jurisdiction, not just from the seller's word, since regulations and caps change and a listing's current status can lapse.

Round out the checklist with a standard home inspection, an HVAC and roof age check, and a furnishings inventory if the sale includes contents -- all of which a done-for-you acquisition process coordinates as part of underwriting a shortlisted property.

Frequently asked

Do I need all of this before I even tour a property?

No -- financial and compliance documents come first since they can disqualify a listing before you spend time touring. Physical inspection happens once a listing has cleared the financial and compliance screen.

What if a seller won't provide platform revenue exports?

That's a significant red flag. A seller with genuinely strong performance has every incentive to share verifiable data; reluctance to provide it is a reason to move on or discount the asking price heavily.
