Buying a short-term rental with existing bookings

A seller’s forward booking calendar is not the same thing as income you can collect after closing. Before you pay for a “booked business,” establish who holds each reservation, which platform rules apply, who owes the guest a stay and where the cash will go. The useful deliverable is a reservation-by-reservation handoff, not a screenshot of a full calendar.

This is an educational buyer workflow, not legal advice or a promise that reservations, permits, reviews or earnings convey with a property. Confirm current platform instructions for the specific transaction and have your attorney, settlement professional and accountant review the agreement and allocations.

Airbnb and Vrbo are not interchangeable

Airbnb: do not assume a reservation transfer

Airbnb’s account-ownership guidance says account ownership and reservations cannot be transferred to another host or account. A co-host appointment or trip-change request does not make a reservation transferable. Do not price the seller’s bookings as your confirmed future revenue or accept account credentials as a handoff solution. Ask Airbnb Support about the specific situation before relying on an operating plan.

Vrbo: a defined transition process, not an automatic sale benefit

Under Vrbo’s Property Sale and Host Transition Policy, affected guests must be notified and offered a full refund; the host initiates cancellation if they choose it. New bookings must end before the expected transfer, with later dates blocked. Where both hosts agree to honor existing stays and guests keep them, the new host creates a new listing and both hosts contact Support to transfer reservations. Terms must remain the same or improve. Listing content does not transfer; property reviews may transfer on request. Hosts arrange the division of already-paid funds themselves. Sale-related cancellations can carry fees and consequences.

Keep these channel-specific findings alongside the evidence file rather than treating a purchase-contract sentence as platform approval. Direct bookings and other channels need their own contract and policy review. An entry on a property-management calendar may be a synchronized copy, an owner hold or a maintenance block, not another paid stay.

Build a booking register before the diligence deadline

Request a dated, redacted export for stays that overlap or follow the proposed closing date. Begin with booking references rather than guest names or contact information. Restrict access to underlying guest records and obtain only what is necessary and permitted for the review. Compare the register with the seller’s current channel records; a screenshot does not show the latest cancellation, refund or payout.

Use separate status columns for the reservation, the money and the operating handoff. “Confirmed booking” does not establish “buyer receives payout.” “Seller paid” does not establish “funds credited at closing.” “Guest notified” does not establish “guest accepted.” If those distinctions disappear into one green checkmark, the file is not ready for a purchase decision.

Worked example: do not count the same dollars twice

Suppose a fictional reservation includes $2,000 of accommodation, $200 of cleaning and $240 of lodging tax. The guest-facing total is $2,440. Assume, only for this example, that the platform remits the $240 tax and deducts a $60 host fee, leaving a $2,140 payout. The buyer’s model must not call the full $2,440 accommodation revenue.

If a supported arrangement makes the buyer responsible for the stay and a $200 cleaner bill, $1,940 remains from the $2,140 payout before other operating costs. That is arithmetic, not the buyer’s guaranteed entitlement. The actual allocation depends on the reservation records, payment timing and signed agreement. Actual platform fee and tax treatment may be different.

If the seller already received the $2,140 and a negotiated settlement credit passes that amount to the buyer, do not also forecast a second $2,140 platform receipt. If the payout is still pending, a settlement adjustment and later receipt need a clear reconciliation rule. Separately decide who funds refunds, chargebacks and damage claims and whether a holdback is appropriate; ask the closing professionals to draft those terms.

Ask for five decisions in the purchase file

  1. Which stays are in scope? Attach the dated register, define the cut-off and identify who updates it for changes before closing. A floating reference to “all future bookings” leaves too much unresolved.
  2. Which party performs each obligation? Identify guest support, cleaning, maintenance and emergency coverage. For a stay spanning closing, record the changeover time and who handles any complaint already open.
  3. What happens to the money? Reconcile collected deposits, pending payments, fees and taxes. Have the professionals identify the permitted settlement treatment rather than informally treating every deposit as sale proceeds.
  4. What if the arrangement fails? Ask counsel to address a guest cancellation, unsupported platform transition, delayed closing or refund after the seller has been paid. Do not assume a seller credit removes platform obligations.
  5. When may the buyer rely on the income? Use a clear evidence threshold: channel-specific confirmation, applicable guest choice, payment allocation and an operational plan. Unresolved bookings remain a scenario, not committed buyer cash.

Model an opening period without inherited bookings

Run a separate launch case in which none of the seller’s forward reservations becomes buyer revenue. Include fixed carrying costs, cleaning and setup, any approved refunds or adjustments the buyer must bear, and time needed to make the new operation ready. Do not simply fill the gap with the seller’s average occupancy: a new listing and a changed operator have a different evidence base.

Compare the cash required by that case with the liquidity actually left after closing and launch work. A buyer can decide that the property still fits without inherited bookings, or that the purchase depends on unresolved assumptions. Both conclusions are more useful than a headline “booked revenue” number that cannot be traced to buyer receipts. Use the buyer financial worksheets for a separate carrying-cost scenario.

Closing-day and first-week verification

  • Refresh the register against the channels and compare it with the version attached to the agreement. Flag new cancellations, altered dates and payment changes.
  • Check calendar availability across systems to avoid double booking. Do not delete accounts or cancel reservations as a substitute for a supported transition.
  • Confirm that the actual operator has the necessary insurance, local permissions and service coverage. Platform handling does not establish legal rental use.
  • Verify access and emergency contact arrangements without circulating passwords or guest data in a general purchase spreadsheet.
  • Assign a date to reconcile actual payouts and settlement adjustments. Keep unresolved differences open until the relevant records agree.
