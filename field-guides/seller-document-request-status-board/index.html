Buyer evidence and purchase handoffs · Field guide · October 9, 2026

Track seller document requests by decision deadline

A buyer may request many records without knowing which unresolved item affects the next decision. A status board connects each request to the issue it addresses and the deadline that matters. Keep the board separate from contractual deadlines and have the responsible professionals confirm any legal consequences.

Work through the decision

  1. Tie each request to a decision.
  2. Record date requested and received.
  3. Identify incomplete responses.
  4. Assign follow-up before the relevant decision date.

For each action, name the person responsible for preparing the response and the person who approves it. Record what evidence would close the item. If new information changes cost, timing or scope, update the affected plan and identify who needs the revised version.

Common failure to avoid

A document marked received may still be incomplete or cover the wrong period. Record review status separately from receipt status.

Worked example

A hypothetical buyer receives revenue records for only six months after requesting a full year. The board records the partial response, identifies the missing period and keeps the annual revenue question open.

This is an illustrative workflow, not a client case study or a forecast. Replace the example’s facts with the records for your own project.

Close the handoff

A finished worksheet should identify the result, its evidence and any remaining owner. If the result is uncertain, record the next action instead of marking the whole task complete. Keep the worksheet with the relevant source file and tell the receiving person which version applies.
