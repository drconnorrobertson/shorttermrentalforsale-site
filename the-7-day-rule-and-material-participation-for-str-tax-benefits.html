The 7-Day Rule and Material Participation for STR Tax Benefits

Published 2026-09-25 · Updated 2026-11-21 · BNB Accelerator Editorial Team

Keep the stay calculation and participation review separate

A short-stay listing does not establish a deductible loss. First determine the activity’s treatment under the passive-activity rules, then evaluate participation and the other limitations that apply to the return. Keep the property’s operating budget viable without an assumed tax benefit while those questions remain unresolved.

Treasury Regulation 1.469-1T(e)(3) includes an exception to the section 469 rental-activity definition when the average period of customer use is seven days or less. That is a rule for this tax framework, not a conclusion about local licensing, lodging taxes, self-employment tax or every other treatment of the property. A reservation minimum or marketing label does not establish the annual average.

Worked example: longer stays change the answer

For one fictional property, assume 20 completed rental periods of three days each and five completed rental periods of 28 days each. The first group contributes 60 rental days, the second 140. The calculation uses all 200 days across 25 rental periods: 200 ÷ 25 = eight days. This example does not meet the seven-day exception merely because most reservations are short.

A calendar export is a starting record, not the tax conclusion. Ask the preparer how extensions, cancellations, customer-use periods and any multiple classes of property should be handled. Do not count a maintenance block or an owner stay as another paid reservation to force a preferred average. Retain the original records and document any reconciliation adjustments.

More than 100 hours is only part of one test

Treasury Regulation 1.469-5T(a)(3) requires more than 100 hours and participation at least as much as any other individual for that test. Other tests exist, including more than 500 hours and substantially all participation. These alternatives have their own requirements; they are not interchangeable labels.

Assume, solely for illustration, that an advisor has determined all listed hours are countable for the same activity and year. An owner’s 140 hours do not satisfy test (3) if another individual has 160 hours. An owner’s exactly 100 hours also fails its “more than 100” threshold. Neither example determines whether another test applies or whether the eventual loss is usable.

Request individual-level information where the relevant comparison requires it. A single invoice from a management company does not tell you how much each person participated. Do not invent allocations between workers or treat a company’s aggregated total as a verified individual total. Record missing evidence as missing.

Build an evidence file rather than a target-hour tally

IRS Publication 925 explains the participation tests, exclusions for certain investor work, spouse participation and reasonable ways to establish the work performed. Contemporaneous daily logs are not the only permitted proof. A useful practical file nevertheless connects the task, approximate time, property or activity and supporting record so a preparer can review what counts.

  • Describe the work: write what was done, by whom and when. Separate operating work from reviewing an investment or planning an acquisition.
  • Connect evidence: retain the relevant work order, message, calendar entry or invoice reference in your own secure system. Avoid circulating guest names and contact information in a general worksheet.
  • Separate recorded and accepted time: retain the original entry even when the advisor excludes or adjusts it. Keep the reason and reviewer beside the adjustment.
  • Reconcile the annual scope: use consistent activity and tax-year boundaries. Ask the preparer to evaluate any grouping or ownership questions rather than casually pooling unrelated properties.
  • Record other participants: request support appropriate to the selected test. A staffing change during the year can make an earlier estimate stale.

For example, a fictional entry might say “reviewed and resolved an access-system fault; 0.8 hours; maintenance ticket reference; tax treatment not yet reviewed.” That is more useful than “one hour toward qualification.” The file should describe actual work and preserve uncertainty, not reverse-engineer a threshold after year end.

Do not turn classification into a promised offset

Ask the tax professional to assess basis, at-risk, passive-activity, personal-use and other applicable restrictions before estimating a current-year benefit. Keep the supported depreciation calculation, passive-activity conclusion and usable deduction as separate outputs. None is established by purchasing an LLC, hiring an acquisition team or completing an online calculator.

Use the cost-segregation basis review for the depreciation evidence and the cash-flow downside example for operating liquidity. Review the booking-handoff guide before assuming a seller’s future reservations will become buyer income.
