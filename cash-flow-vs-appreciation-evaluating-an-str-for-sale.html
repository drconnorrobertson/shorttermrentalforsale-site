Cash Flow vs Appreciation: Evaluating an STR For Sale

Published 2026-04-10 · Updated 2026-11-21 · BNB Accelerator Editorial Team

Separate spendable cash from a future sale assumption

A rental can generate positive operating income and still require owner cash after debt payments and replacements. A property can also rise in estimated value while its owner struggles to pay bills. Start with two separate schedules: cash required during ownership and cash received only if a sale actually closes. Appreciation cannot fund this month’s expenses without a sale or additional borrowing, each of which has costs and uncertainty.

Use the same purchase assumptions when comparing the two strategies. State the holding period, total entry cash, recurring costs, financing payments, replacement allowance and sale assumptions. Then ask whether the property can remain financially supportable if value is flat or falls. An attractive resale scenario does not establish the ability to hold through a difficult year.

A fictional annual operating bridge

The following example is invented to explain the calculation. It is not a listing, market estimate, financing quote or client result. Assume a $500,000 purchase, $125,000 down payment, $15,000 closing allowance, $30,000 furnishing and launch budget, and $20,000 retained reserve. Total entry cash is $190,000. The reserve remains cash held for the property; it is not an expense deduction.

The $3,000 base cash flow is approximately 1.6% of $190,000 entry cash. It is not $30,000 of spendable income. The downside requires a $6,000 owner contribution for the year, before an unexpected repair. Variable costs fall with revenue in this example; fixed bills, debt and the replacement allowance do not. Real costs may behave differently. Revenue excludes guest cleaning charges and lodging taxes; income taxes, appreciation and sale proceeds are outside this operating bridge.

Find the cash threshold before predicting demand

Under these assumptions, revenue must cover $42,000 of fixed bills, debt and replacement funding after 25% variable costs. The cash break-even revenue is $42,000 ÷ 0.75 = $56,000. At an assumed $250 per paid night and 365 available nights, that requires 224 paid nights, or approximately 61.4% paid occupancy. Those are mathematical thresholds, not evidence that guests will book those nights. Owner stays, closures, discounts, refunds and seasonality require separate adjustments.

Obtain a monthly schedule as well as an annual total. A positive year can contain several months requiring cash. Keep the reserve available for that timing gap and assess it against actual fixed commitments, known work and household liquidity. Do not count the same reserve as both cash invested and money already spent on improvements.

Keep the resale calculation separate

For a separate fictional exit illustration, assume a $350,000 remaining loan payoff and selling costs equal to 7% of the sale price. A $550,000 sale leaves $161,500 after $38,500 selling costs and loan payoff. A flat $500,000 sale leaves $115,000; a $450,000 sale leaves $68,500. These amounts exclude taxes, additional closing adjustments and any other liabilities. The assumed payoff is an input, not a prediction of an amortization schedule.

None of those figures is total profit or a return on entry cash. A full holding-period reconciliation must also include operating cash received or contributed, later capital spending, financing changes, taxes and unused reserves returned. Principal payments reduce operating cash and may reduce the eventual loan payoff; do not add principal reduction again when sale proceeds already use that reduced payoff.

Build a decision file that can change your answer

  • Revenue evidence: reconcile the seller’s records using the seller revenue verification guide. Separate recorded history from projections and transferable bookings.
  • Operating commitments: replace allowances with property-specific insurance, maintenance, management and utility evidence. Record quote dates and exclusions.
  • Financing: use the actual payment schedule, maturity, adjustable terms and fees. An annual payment assumption does not establish loan availability.
  • Exit uncertainty: document why an exit price is being tested and include flat and falling values. A future refinance is a scenario until available on acceptable terms.
  • Permission and timing: use the rental-use review questions and assess a launch period without inherited income. Read the booking-handoff guide before treating a seller’s calendar as buyer cash.

Choose a property only after comparing the required contributions and downside with your actual capacity to hold it. Use the editable financial worksheets to test inputs, then retain the evidence and unresolved questions beside the result.
