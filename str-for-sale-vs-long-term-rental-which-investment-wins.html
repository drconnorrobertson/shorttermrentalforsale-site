STR For Sale vs Long-Term Rental: Which Investment Wins

Published 2026-07-19 · Short Term Rental For Sale Editorial

Income potential and volatility

A well-located short-term rental typically has higher gross income potential than the same property leased long-term, because nightly rates during peak demand periods can exceed what a twelve-month lease would ever capture. That upside comes with real revenue volatility across seasons that a long-term lease simply doesn't have.

A long-term rental produces a flatter, more predictable income line -- lower ceiling, but also a much higher floor.

Effort, vacancy risk, and tax treatment differ meaningfully

Short-term rentals require active management -- or paid management -- and carry turnover, cleaning, and guest-communication overhead that a long-term lease doesn't. They also carry a different tax profile: the average-stay rules that can make losses more usable against other income for a materially participating owner don't apply to a standard long-term lease.

Neither structure is universally better -- the right answer depends on the owner's time, market, and tax situation, which is exactly what a proper acquisition consultation should walk through before a purchase.
